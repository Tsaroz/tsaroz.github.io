## Why buy an electric scooter in Nepal in 2026?

The maths has shifted decisively in favour of electric. A petrol scooter costs roughly NPR 3–4 per kilometre to run once you factor in fuel and servicing; an e-scooter costs a fraction of that on home charging. Add lower registration taxes on EVs, no engine oil, no spark plugs and far fewer moving parts to wear out, and the total cost of ownership over five years is compelling.

The catch is that Nepal's terrain and road conditions are uniquely demanding. Steep climbs, dusty streets, monsoon flooding and long gaps between service points all stress a scooter in ways a spec sheet never shows. That is why this guide weights **real-world range**, **ground clearance**, **service network** and **build quality** as heavily as headline numbers.

> Claimed range is measured on a flat test track at a constant speed. Expect 25–35% less on Kathmandu's hills with a passenger on board. We list realistic ranges, not brochure figures.

## The best electric scooters in Nepal, reviewed

### 1. Niu NQi — best overall

![Niu NQi electric scooter](/images/scooters/nio-es8.svg)

The Niu NQi is the scooter we recommend to most people. Its 1200W motor pulls cleanly up Kathmandu's inclines, the lithium battery delivers a realistic 70–90 km, and the ride quality is a step above the budget crowd thanks to better suspension and tyres. The companion app adds useful touches like battery health, ride history and GPS anti-theft.

Where it wins is refinement. Everything from the throttle response to the seat comfort feels considered. If you can stretch to its price, it is the least compromised option in this list.

**Best for:** daily commuters who want one scooter to do everything well.

### 2. Yadea G5 — best practical commuter

![Yadea G5 electric scooter](/images/scooters/yadea-g5.svg)

Yadea is one of the world's largest e-scooter makers, and the G5 shows why. It is comfortable, has a generous seat and boot, and its dealer network in Nepal is growing quickly — important when you need a service or a spare part.

Range is a realistic 60–80 km and the 1000W motor is relaxed rather than thrilling, which suits city riding. It is not the cheapest, but it is one of the easiest to live with.

**Best for:** first-time EV buyers who value reliability and support.

### 3. Aeon Ai-3 — best value

![Aeon Ai-3 electric scooter](/images/scooters/aeon-ai-3.svg)

The Ai-3 undercuts the premium brands by a meaningful margin while still offering a 55–70 km range and a comfortable ride. It is a sensible middle ground: cheaper than the Niu and Yadea, better finished than the budget options.

The trade-offs are a slightly plasticky finish and a smaller boot. Neither is a dealbreaker at this price.

**Best for:** buyers who want good range without paying premium-brand prices.

### 4. TVS iQube — best for peace of mind

![TVS iQube electric scooter](/images/scooters/tvs-iqube.svg)

TVS is a household name in Nepal, and the iQube leverages that with the widest service network on this list. Its 3.4 kWh battery and 4400W peak motor give it the strongest performance here — a top speed of 78 km/h and a claimed 75–100 km range.

It costs the most, but you are paying for a proven drivetrain and the confidence that a service centre is never far away.

**Best for:** riders who prioritise performance and after-sales support.

### 5. Pure EV EPluto 7G — best budget pick

![Pure EV EPluto 7G electric scooter](/images/scooters/pure-ev-ecute.svg)

At the most affordable end of the credible market, the EPluto 7G delivers a 50–65 km range and a 1200W motor for short money. It is ideal for short daily trips around town — school runs, errands, the office commute — where its limited top speed and smaller boot do not matter.

If your daily distance is under 40 km and you charge at home, it is excellent value.

**Best for:** budget-conscious riders with short daily trips.

## What to check before you buy

- **Service centre distance.** Ask where the nearest authorised workshop is. A great scooter with no support nearby is a liability.
- **Battery warranty.** Look for at least 3 years on the battery, with a clear capacity-retention guarantee.
- **Charging setup.** Most scooters charge from a standard 5A socket, but confirm your building's wiring and parking can handle a 5–7 hour charge.
- **Real range test.** Ask the dealer for a test ride on a route similar to your commute, ideally with a passenger.
- **Registration and insurance.** Confirm the dealer handles EV registration and that insurance covers the battery.
- **Spare parts availability.** Ask specifically about tyres, brake pads and controllers — the parts that wear or fail first.
